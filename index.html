Stop paying five figures for free photos.

Hey there—

Here’s something that bugs me.

Every day, visitors post thousands of beautiful photos of your destination. For free. Because they had a great time.

Then a DMO pays a software company $10,000 or more a year to ask permission to use them.

Sit with that for a second. The photos are free. The permission is a comment reply. The bill is five figures.

That money comes from somewhere. Usually from the same budget meant to promote the places visitors fell in love with. The bakery. The outfitter. The inn with the good porch.

And here’s the worse part. Those businesses rarely see the photos. The library sits with the DMO. The galleries sit on the DMO’s website. Meanwhile the outfitter is still shooting on a cracked phone or paying for stock.

That’s backwards. A destination isn’t a website. It’s a few hundred businesses doing good work. The photos people take of those businesses should help those businesses.

So we built ShareWhere. It does the job for a fraction of the price, and it puts the photos where they do the most good.

How it works

Four steps to become a DMO hero.

  1. 1

    Find.

    We pull public posts tagged at your destination, from Instagram and TikTok. AI sorts them by place, season and activity, so the good stuff rises to the top.

  2. 2

    Ask.

    One click sends a friendly permission request. The creator replies yes. We keep the record: who agreed, when, and to what.

  3. 3

    Use.

    Drop galleries on your website. Build a feed of visitor videos. Download for social, print and ads. Every photo carries its creator’s credit.

  4. 4

    Share.

    The permission covers your member businesses too. The inn gets the photos of the inn. The outfitter gets the paddlers. The bakery gets the croissant someone couldn’t stop photographing. Each one gets a gallery for its own website, too.

Every business gets its own library.

A simple page with the cleared photos of their place, ready to post. No training session. They don’t pay a cent. You do, and it’s still a fraction of what you pay now.

It makes the DMO look good where it counts. Your members see the value of their membership every time they open their library. Their profile goes up. So does the destination’s.
